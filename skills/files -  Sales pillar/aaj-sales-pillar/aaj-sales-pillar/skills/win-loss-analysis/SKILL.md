# Win-Loss Analysis

Turn scattered deal outcomes into the few patterns that explain most of your wins and losses — and the fixes worth making.

## When to use this
- Closing below target and the reasons aren't clear.
- The same kinds of deals keep slipping away.
- Before changing the pitch, packaging, or process — so the change targets a real pattern.

## When not to use this
- There aren't enough closed deals yet to see a pattern → gather more, and use `discovery-call-framework` to improve the inputs first.
- It's a single high-stakes deal review → this still helps, but treat one deal as a story, not a pattern.

## What you need
A list of recent closed deals — won and lost — with outcome, segment, competitor (if any), and the stage a lost deal died at. Pull ICP, positioning, and competitors from `brand-product-context`. If reasons aren't recorded, run quick win-loss interviews (3–4 questions: why us / why not / who else / what tipped it).

## Method
1. **Assemble the set.** Label each deal won/lost, segment, competitor, and the stage it was lost at.
2. **Code the reasons** into a small, consistent set (price, product gap, timing, champion lost, lost to Competitor X, no decision) — don't let every deal have a unique reason.
3. **Rank** loss and win reasons by both frequency *and* revenue weight; they often differ.
4. **Cut by segment and competitor.** Patterns usually diverge (lose on price in SMB, win on depth in mid-market).
5. **Split fixable-by-us** (pitch, process, packaging) from market reality (no budget, bad fit). Focus on the first.
6. **Translate** the top 2–3 patterns into specific fixes, each owned by product, pitch/marketing, or sales process.

## Output
- **Top win reasons** — ranked, with frequency and revenue weight.
- **Top loss reasons** — ranked the same way.
- **Patterns by segment and by competitor.**
- **No-decision rate** — called out separately (losing to "nothing" is a different problem than losing to a rival).
- **Prioritized fixes** — each tagged product / pitch / process, with the pattern it addresses and the share of deals it would affect.

## Defensibility check
Confirm reasons are coded from real deal evidence, not anecdote; the sample is big enough to mean something (say so plainly if it isn't); and each fix names the pattern and the rough share of deals it touches. Flag thin samples rather than over-reading them.
