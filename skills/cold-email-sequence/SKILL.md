# Cold Email Sequence

Write a short outbound sequence that earns a reply by leading with the prospect's problem — not your product.

## When to use this
- Opening conversations with a target account list.
- Current outreach gets ignored.
- Launching a new outbound motion.

## When not to use this
- The account list / ICP isn't set → run `target-account-list` and `brand-product-context` first.
- The user needs to nurture existing leads → that's a lifecycle sequence, a different intent.

## What you need
The ICP and its single sharpest pain, plus the differentiator and one piece of proof, from `brand-product-context`. Also: who's being emailed (the role) and the relevance hook that triggered the outreach (a signal, an event, a shared context).

## Method
1. **Anchor every email on one specific pain** the role feels — open in their world, not with your company.
2. **One email, one idea, one ask.** The ask is low-friction (a question, a small yes), not "book a 30-minute demo" on the first touch.
3. **Keep it short** — 50–90 words, plain text, no jargon. Write like a person.
4. **Use proof sparingly** — one concrete outcome or number beats a pile of adjectives.
5. **Vary the angle across touches** (pain → proof → a different pain → break-up); don't just "bumping this up" the same thread.
6. **End with a respectful break-up** — it often pulls the reply the rest couldn't.

## Output
- **4–6 emails**, each with: a subject line (short, lowercase, relevance or curiosity — no clickbait), the body, and the single ask.
- **Send timing** (e.g., day 1, 3, 6, 10, 14).
- The **pain/angle** each touch uses.
- **2–3 alternate first-touch subject lines** to test.

## Defensibility check
Confirm each email leads with the prospect's problem, carries exactly one ask, stays under ~90 words, and makes no claim that isn't backed by real proof from context. If an email is about you instead of them, rewrite it.
