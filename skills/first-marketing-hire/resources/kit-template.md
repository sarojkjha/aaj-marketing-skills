# First Marketing Hire Kit

_Role scorecard, paid take-home, interview questions, first 90 days and the offer_

I wrote this kit for founders hiring their first marketer, from seed to Series B. In my view, most first-hire mistakes come from hiring before you know what the job is: a senior leader who expects a team and a budget, or a junior coordinator who expects direction. The kit makes you write the job down first, then tests every candidate against the same page.

Use the sections in order. Section 1 and Section 2 are written before you post the role. Section 3 runs during the interviews. Section 4 is shared with your chosen candidate before they sign. Section 5 is the check before the offer goes out.

> How this template is marked up. Rows that begin "e.g." are one worked example carried through the whole kit: Example Co., a fictional seed-stage B2B SaaS startup that automates the month-end close for finance teams. Example Co. is AAJ's running case, not a client, and every name, number and rating in it is illustrative. Delete those rows and write your own. Empty rows are yours to fill in. ## Before you start: check there is something to amplify

Hire when something is already working and founder time is the only thing stopping you from doing more of it. Write your evidence for each signal. If one is missing, fix that first; a hire will not fix it for you.

| Signal | Your evidence | True? |
|---|---|---|
| e.g. Retention in one segment | 13 of 14 customers still active, all finance teams at companies with 50 to 500 employees | Yes |
| e.g. One channel that works | Accounting communities brought in 9 of the 14 customers | Yes |
| e.g. Founder time is the constraint | The CEO spends about 15 hours a week on posts and community answers and cannot give more | Yes |
| Retention in one segment: customers in at least one segment keep using and paying |  |  |
| One channel that works: one source produces customers at a cost you can afford |  |  |
| Founder time is the constraint: the marketing that works cannot get more of your time |  |  |

**Finish this sentence: "We have [what exists], and we are missing [what does not]." The missing half is the job.**

> e.g. "We have positioning the CEO wrote and a channel that works, and we are missing the time to run it." So Example Co. hires a hands-on marketer and the CEO keeps the strategy.

### 1c. Competencies, weights and must-haves

### The rating scale and the rules

| Score | Meaning | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Strong no | Clear evidence they cannot do this |
| 2 | No | Some evidence, not enough for this role |
| 3 | Yes | Evidence they can do this at the level the role needs |
| 4 | Strong yes | Evidence they have done more than this role needs |

- There is no middle score. A 1 to 4 scale makes every interviewer come down on one side.
- Every rating needs an evidence note: what the candidate said or did. A rating without one is an opinion and does not count.
- A must-have needs an average of 3 or more across interviewers (its bar), unless you set a different bar above.
- The hire line is a weighted average of 3.0. A candidate below it is a no, even with every must-have met.
- When two interviewers are 2 or more points apart on one competency, they saw different people. Talk before you decide.
- Average each interviewer first, so someone who rated a competency at three stages does not outvote the rest of the panel.

> These are AAJ's rules, not research findings. Change them if you have a reason, but change them before the first interview, not after you have a favourite.

> Read the calls in this order. A must-have below its bar, with the panel agreeing, is a no-hire whatever the total. A must-have with no evidence, or with interviewers 2 or more apart, is more evidence. Then the weighted score against the hire line. If nobody clears the bar, re-open the search rather than lower it.

## 2. The paid take-home task

A short paid project on your real situation tells you more than another interview. Write the brief before you post the role, so every finalist gets the same test, and write the scoring rubric before you read any answers.

- Pay for it, and pay every finalist the same fee. I would not ask anyone to do unpaid work on a real problem.
- Cap it in hours, and say the cap in the brief. You are testing how they reason, not how many evenings they have.
- Use your real situation, with the data you can share. A generic case tells you who is good at generic cases.
- Look for how they reason about your customer and your numbers, not polish.
- Say in writing who owns the work and whether you will use it. How you pay a candidate who is not yet an employee is a tax and employment question; check it with your accountant or counsel.

### 2b. The scoring rubric

Each criterion maps to a competency in 1c, so the take-home review feeds the same scorecard. Score it before the debrief, then again after it if the debrief changes your view, with the reason.

| Criterion | Comp. | 1 (strong no) | 2 (no) | 3 (yes) | 4 (strong yes) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Understands your customer | C2 | Writes for a generic market | Names the buyer but not why they buy | Separates who signs and who uses, and says why each would buy | Spots something about your buyer you had not written down |
| Priorities | C6 | Proposes everything | Ranks, but without reasons | Picks one place to start and says why | Says what to stop doing to make room, and what that costs |
| A test plan with a measurable pass mark | C3 | No way to tell if it worked | Measures activity, not results | Each test has a pass mark you could check with your tracking | Pass marks, stop rules, and what the result would change |
| Grows the working channel | C1 | Ignores the channel that works | Does more of the same | Specific changes to the working channel, with reasons | Changes grounded in your numbers, with the first two weeks spelled out |
| Adapts in the debrief | C6 | Defends the plan | Adjusts only when pushed | Changes the plan sensibly when one assumption changes | Changes it and says which part of the original plan was weakest |

> In the 30-minute debrief, change one assumption and watch how they adapt. e.g. "Suppose the CFO, not the controller, starts the search. What changes?"

## 3. Interview questions by stage

Every question is tied to a competency in 1c, so every answer becomes a rating with an evidence note. Ask every candidate the same questions at each stage. Add your own, but tie each one to a competency or drop it.

> Keep every question to the job. Do not ask about age, family plans, health, religion, national origin or any other protected characteristic. The US Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) and your state's employment agency publish what employers may not ask, the rules differ by state and country, and you should check them with counsel before you interview.

### Stage 1: Founder screen (30 minutes, a founder)

_Is this person worth a practitioner's hour? Test the deep skill at the level of the story, and set honest expectations about the deal._

| # | Question | Comp. | Listen for | Red flag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Walk me through one channel you grew: the starting numbers, what you changed, the result, and who else did the work. | C1 | Numbers they can show; their own part separated from the team's | Only outcomes, no mechanics; "we" with no "I" |
| 2 | From what you have seen of us, who signs for a product like ours, who uses it, and what would make each of them say no? | C2 | Separates buyer and user; asks questions back | Describes a market, not a person |
| 3 | In your last role, what did you do yourself each week, and what did you hand to someone else? | C4 | Work they shipped with their own hands | Everything went through an agency or a team |
| 4 | If you could do only one thing in your first month here, what would it be, and what would you not do? | C6 | Picks one thing and gives something up | Plans to do everything at once |

### Stage 2: Practitioner deep-dive (60 minutes, someone who has run the channel)

_A founder who has not run the channel cannot judge depth in it. Ask a fractional leader, an advisor or a marketer you trust to run this stage._

| # | Question | Comp. | Listen for | Red flag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Show me the numbers from that channel. What did a good week look like, and what moved it? | C1 | Leading indicators and the mechanics behind them | Only likes, impressions or followers |
| 6 | Was the channel working when you arrived? What was there before you, and what did you build? | C1 | A clear before and after that they caused | Ran a channel someone else built and calls it growth |
| 7 | Tell me about a test that failed. What was the pass mark, and what did you do next? | C3 | A pass mark set before launch; a decision taken on it | No failed tests, or a pass mark set after the result |
| 8 | How did you know which leads came from which source at your last company? What did you set up yourself? | C5 | Tracking parameters, a CRM source field, form fields they set up | "The ops team handled that" |
| 9 | What have you done in [your bonus skill], and what would you still need to learn? | C7 | Specific work, and honest limits | Claims depth in everything |

### Stage 3: Take-home review (30 to 45 minutes, a founder and one other)

_Score the work against the rubric in 2b first, then use the debrief to test how they think._

| # | Question | Comp. | Listen for | Red flag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | Walk me through who this plan is for, and why them first. | C2 | The buyer named precisely, with the reason | The plan is for "SMBs" or "finance" |
| 11 | Which test would you run first, and what result would make you stop it? | C3 | A stop rule they can state without notes | Would keep going and see |
| 12 | What did you leave out, and what does leaving it out cost us? | C6 | A named trade-off and its cost | Nothing was left out |
| 13 | Suppose [one assumption changes]. What changes in your plan? | C6 | Adapts the plan and says which part was weakest | Defends the original |

### Stage 4: References (2 or 3 calls of 20 minutes)

_Ask the candidate for a former manager and a peer, and get their permission before you call anyone. Spend most of each call on the deep skill._

| # | Question | Comp. | Listen for | Red flag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | Did [name] grow a channel, or run one someone else built? What were the numbers before and after? | C1 | Numbers that match the candidate's story | A different story, or vague praise |
| 15 | How well did [name] know your customers? Did they talk to them? | C2 | Regular customer contact | Worked from briefs only |
| 16 | What did [name] do with their own hands each week? | C4 | Shipped work, not only managed work | Mostly coordinated others |
| 17 | Tell me about a time [name] argued for a trade-off or said no to something. | C6 | A specific example | Cannot think of one |

## 4. The first 90 days: diagnose, design, execute

AAJ's method is Diagnose, Design, Execute, and the first 90 days follow it: learn, fix the foundations, then grow one channel. Agree the day-90 targets in writing and share them with your chosen candidate before they sign, so both of you know what success looks like.

| Phase | Goal | What the hire does | What the founder does | Done by the end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Days 1-30: Diagnose | Learn the customer, the numbers and what is broken | Talks to ten customers; sits in on sales calls; audits tracking and the website; reads every win and loss note; maps where the last 10 to 20 customers came from | Opens doors: customer intros, CRM, analytics, ad accounts; one 30-minute check-in a week | A one-page diagnosis: what works, what is broken, the constraint |
| Days 31-60: Design | Fix the foundations and agree the plan | Agrees positioning and messaging with the founder; fixes tracking so results can be measured; agrees lead stages with sales; designs the experiments with pass marks | Agrees the targets and the budget; signs off the messaging | Tracking that works, written targets, an experiment plan |
| Days 61-90: Execute | Grow one channel | Runs the experiments on the working channel; reports weekly; proposes the next channel to test | Reviews the weekly report; decides on the next channel | Results against the pass marks, and a plan for the next quarter |

### Day-90 targets (agree these on day one)

| Target | How it is measured | Today | By day 90 |
|---|---|---|---|
| e.g. Lead stages and a lead score in place | Agreed with sales, in the CRM | None | Live |
| e.g. Tracking fixed | Share of new leads with a known source | [Today's share] | [Target share] |
| e.g. Experiments in accounting communities | Run against a pass mark set in advance | 0 | 3 |
| e.g. Marketing-qualified leads a month | From the CRM, by source | [Today] | About 36 (fictional) |
|   |   |   |   |
|   |   |   |   |

### The three check-ins

- Day 30: Is the diagnosis right? What surprised them? What do they need from you?
- Day 60: Are the targets still the right ones? Is tracking good enough to judge the experiments?
- Day 90: Results against the targets. What next quarter looks like. Whether the role, as written, is still the right one.

## 5. The offer-stage checklist

Tick every line before the offer goes out. The ones about pay, equity and checks are prompts to get advice, not advice.

- [ ] Every interviewer's ratings are in, each with an evidence note (Hiring founder)
- [ ] Every must-have is at or above its bar, and any disagreement of 2 or more has been talked through (Hiring founder)
- [ ] References done, at least one on the deep skill: did they grow a channel, or run one someone else built? (Hiring founder)
- [ ] The take-home fee has been paid (Finance)
- [ ] The day-90 targets are written and shared with the candidate (Hiring founder)
- [ ] The offer is honest about the deal: who they report to, the team they will (not yet) have, and the budget you have actually raised (Hiring founder)
- [ ] Cash pay set from public wage data for where the person will work (for example, US Bureau of Labor Statistics state and area wage data), adjusted for your stage (Hiring founder)
- [ ] Equity explained in writing: the grant, vesting, cliff, and what happens if they leave. Have counsel or your equity administrator check the terms (Counsel)
- [ ] Employment status, offer letter and any pay-range disclosure rules where the person will work checked with counsel; rules differ by state and country (Counsel)
- [ ] If you use a background-check company, the federal and state rules that apply checked with counsel first (Counsel)
- [ ] The company owns the accounts the hire will run: ad accounts, CRM, analytics, domains, social profiles (Hiring founder)
- [ ] Week one planned: customers to call, sales calls to join, people to meet (Hiring founder)
- [ ] Day-30, day-60 and day-90 check-ins in both calendars (Hiring founder)
- [ ] The other finalists told promptly, with a line of feedback on their take-home (Hiring founder)
