# Positioning frameworks & output

## Core frame (use this first)

> For **[target customer]** who **[need / problem]**, **[product]** is a **[category]** that **[single most important benefit]**. Unlike **[primary competitive alternative]**, **[product]** **[key differentiator]**.

Keep each slot concrete:
- **Target** — a specific, recognizable segment (role + context), not "businesses."
- **Need** — the problem in the customer's words, not a feature gap.
- **Category** — the frame of reference the buyer already understands (or one you're deliberately creating).
- **Benefit** — one outcome, the most important one. Not a list.
- **Alternative** — what they use today, including spreadsheets, manual work, or "do nothing."
- **Differentiator** — something the alternative cannot equally claim, ideally provable.

## Worked example

> For **B2B SaaS founders running marketing without a CMO** who **are spending on tactics without knowing what's working**, **AAJ** is a **marketing consultancy** that **turns scattered effort into decisions you can defend**. Unlike **generalist agencies that bill for activity**, **AAJ** **ties every recommendation to unit economics and measurable outcomes**.

Proof points: a Diagnose→Design→Execute method; free tools that show the math (allocator, unit-economics, readiness scorer); founding-client case results.

One-line value proposition: *"Sharper strategy. Measurable growth."*

## Pressure-test (rewrite anything that fails)

- **Specific?** Could a competitor swap their name in and have it still ring true? If yes, it's too generic.
- **Differentiated?** Is the differentiator something rivals can't equally claim?
- **Defensible?** Can each claim be backed with proof (data, mechanism, track record)?
- **True?** Would a current customer agree, or roll their eyes?
- **Single-minded?** One target, one core benefit — not a committee's wishlist.
- **Plain?** No jargon the target wouldn't use themselves.

## Audience lenses

Hold the core fixed; shift emphasis:
- **Economic buyer** — outcome, ROI, risk reduction, time-to-value.
- **End user** — workflow, ease, what their day feels like with it.
- **Technical evaluator** — capability, integration, security, control.

## Output format

---
**Positioning statement**
> [the full core-frame statement]

**Proof points**
1. [substantiates the benefit]
2. [substantiates the differentiator]
3. [credibility / track record]

**Value proposition (one line)**
> [hero-ready line]

**By audience**
- *Economic buyer:* [one or two sentences]
- *End user:* [one or two sentences]
- *Technical evaluator:* [one or two sentences]

**Claims needing proof**
- [any statement above that isn't yet backed — what evidence would close it]
