# Sales Process Design

Turn an ad-hoc sales motion into defined stages with clear exit criteria — so deals move for real reasons and the pipeline actually means something.

## When to use this
- Pipeline stages are vague or every rep uses them differently.
- Reps disagree on what "qualified" means.
- Forecasts are unreliable because the stages aren't defined.
- The team is scaling and the motion needs to be repeatable.

## When not to use this
- The user needs the forecasting math → use `pipeline-and-forecast`.
- They need the first-call script → use `discovery-call-framework` (this defines the stages those calls live inside).

## What you need
How deals actually flow today, the average deal size and sales cycle, who's involved (SDR / AE / SE), and the ICP from `brand-product-context`.

## Method
1. **Map the buyer's journey first** — how customers actually decide and buy — then design stages to mirror it, not your internal steps.
2. **Give every stage a verifiable exit criterion** — something objective that must be true to advance ("economic buyer confirmed," "technical validation complete"), never a feeling.
3. **Pick a qualification framework** sized to the deal (lightweight for SMB, MEDDIC-style for enterprise) and bake its checks into the exit criteria.
4. **Assign owner actions** per stage: what the rep does, and what the buyer does to advance.
5. **Set stall rules** — a default "age-out" for deals that stop moving.
6. **Keep it few and sharp** — 4–6 stages is usually right.

## Output
- The **stage list**, each with: entry trigger, exit criterion, owner actions, typical duration.
- The **qualification framework** and where each check is satisfied.
- **Stall / age-out rules.**
- A one-line definition of a **"qualified opportunity."**

## Defensibility check
Confirm every exit criterion is objectively verifiable (could two reps agree it's met?), the stages mirror how buyers actually buy, and the whole thing is simple enough to be followed. Vague exit criteria ("had a good call") are the main failure mode — reject them.
