# Objection Handling

Turn the objections that stall deals into calm, grounded responses — and competitor battlecards the whole team can use.

## When to use this
- The same objections keep killing deals.
- Reps get defensive or discount too fast.
- You're losing to a specific competitor and need a battlecard.

## When not to use this
- The real issue is bad fit — `win-loss-analysis` will show this; handle fit at qualification, not with rebuttals.
- The positioning itself is unclear → run `positioning-statement` first.

## What you need
The real objections (from `win-loss-analysis`, call notes, or the user), the differentiator and proof from `brand-product-context`, and the main competitors.

## Method
1. **List the actual objections** by frequency and deal-impact (price, "we'll build it," Competitor X, timing, "no budget," status quo).
2. **Classify each** — real blocker, misunderstanding, or smokescreen for something else. They need different responses.
3. **Write each response as acknowledge → reframe → prove** — no defensiveness, no trashing competitors.
4. **Anchor in proof and the differentiator**, not opinion.
5. **Build competitor battlecards** — where you genuinely win, where they win (be honest), and the question that reframes to your strength.
6. **Mark the disqualifiers** — objections that mean "walk away," not "overcome."

## Output
- **Top objections**, each with: type, the response (acknowledge → reframe → prove), and the proof it leans on.
- **Competitor battlecards** — honest strengths and weaknesses, plus the reframing question.
- **Disqualify signals** — objections you shouldn't try to overcome.

## Defensibility check
Confirm responses acknowledge the concern honestly, never disparage a competitor or overclaim, and lean on real proof from context. A response that's a clever rebuttal with no substance erodes trust — ground it or cut it.
